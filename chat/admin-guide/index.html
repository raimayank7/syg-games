Admin guide and toolkit

Four short guides and a toolkit of ready messages for the person who keeps the group running.

  • How to be a good group adminTen habits of admins whose groups stay useful, friendly and alive, whether it is a family, society, class or office group.
  • How to stop a fight in a group chatA calm, step-by-step way for admins to stop an argument in a WhatsApp group without taking sides or losing members.
  • How to stop fake forwards in your groupSimple checks for members and firm, polite steps for admins to keep rumours, fake health tips and scam messages out of a group.
  • How to set up a new WhatsApp group properlyA ten-minute checklist for starting a group: name, description, rules, permissions, admins and the first message.
  • Welcome messages for new group membersReady messages to welcome a new member. A good welcome tells the person what the group is for and makes it easy to say hello.

  • Group descriptions to copyShort texts for the "group description" box. A clear description answers the question every new member has: what is this group for?
  • Poll ideas for WhatsApp groupsPolls are the easiest way to wake up a group, because voting takes one tap. Each idea below has a question and its options, ready to type into a WhatsApp poll.

What an admin's job really is

An admin is not the owner of a group and not its police. The job is closer to that of a host: to make sure people know why they are here, to keep the room comfortable, and to step in early, quietly, when two guests begin to quarrel. Most of that work is done by a handful of habits rather than by any setting in the app: a clear purpose, a few pinned rules, a welcome for each newcomer, reminders in public and corrections in private.

Where to begin

If you are starting a group today, follow the set-up checklist first, then pin a set of rules. If you have inherited a noisy group, read the guide on being a good admin and choose one change to make this week; groups accept one change at a time. If a quarrel is already running, go straight to the guide on stopping fights. And if rumours and doubtful health tips are the problem, the guide on fake forwards has polite words for the hardest case of all: correcting an elder.

The toolkit

The ready messages save you from writing the same thing again and again: welcomes for new members, announcements and gentle warnings, one-line group descriptions and polls for decisions. Change the words in brackets and send. They are written to be firm about the matter and kind to the people, which is the whole art of running a group.
