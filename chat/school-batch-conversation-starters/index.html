Conversation starters for old school and college batches

Questions for a batch group, to bring back the classroom, the canteen and the teachers everybody imitated.

Or tap any question to copy it.

Why batch groups need a spark

A batch group starts with a rush: two hundred "long time!" messages in the first week. Then it settles into birthday wishes and silence, because classmates who have not met for fifteen years no longer know each other's present lives. What they do share, completely and for ever, is the past. Questions about that past are the one subject on which every member is an expert, from the topper to the back-bencher.

Getting everyone in, not only the loud ones

Every batch group has ten members who talk and a hundred who watch. To bring in the watchers, ask questions with small, factual answers: who sat next to you in class 8, what was in your tiffin, which bus route did you take. Nobody feels judged answering those. Follow with a request for old photographs, and the group will run on its own for days. Mention quieter classmates by name kindly ("Does anyone remember who won the drawing prize?").

Handle with care

School was not a happy time for everyone. Avoid questions that bring back old nicknames, punishments, failures, who liked whom, or the mocking of any classmate or teacher. Remember too that some batchmates may no longer be alive; if that comes up, pause the game and let people share memories with respect. Do not turn questions into a comparison of jobs, cities or success.

Planning a reunion? Use the poll ideas for dates and the batch group names for a fresh title.

How to get replies

  • Post one question at a time, not a list.
  • Answer it yourself first. A short, honest answer invites others.
  • Choose a time when people are free: Sunday morning for families, lunch break for teams.
  • React to every answer. People reply again when they feel heard.
