Office and team group names

Names for the team group at work. Light enough to make people smile, proper enough for the manager to see.

Tap a name to copy it. Then open your group, tap its name at the top and paste.

Make a name with your own word

Light enough to smile at, proper enough for the manager

An office group name is seen by more people than you expect: it pops up during screen sharing, it sits in the manager's chat list, and a client may glance at your phone. The safe kind of office humour laughs at the work itself, such as deadlines, Mondays, tea breaks and the reply-all button. Everybody from the trainee to the head of department shares those, so nobody feels aimed at.

If the group is the official team channel, put the team or project name first and keep the joke small: "Accounts: The Deadline Diaries". If it is the unofficial lunch or tea group, you have more room, but remember that colleagues change teams and take screenshots with them.

Names an office group should not have

Nothing about the boss, a client, the salary, the appraisal or a colleague, even in code. Nothing that suggests the team is not working ("Timepass Department" reads badly in the wrong meeting). Nothing with rude words or drinking references. And if two groups exist, one with the manager and one without, name them so differently that a message cannot go to the wrong one by mistake; that single error has caused more office embarrassment than any name.

Pair the name with clear office group rules about hours and replies, and use the work-safe conversation starters for the first five minutes of a Monday.

How to choose a good group name

  • Say what the group is. A new member should understand it from the name.
  • Keep it short. Long names are cut off in the chat list; about 25 characters show fully.
  • Make it yours. Add a surname, a city, a year or a flat block: "Sharma Parivaar Express", "Block B Buzz".
  • Keep it kind. A name that makes fun of one member stops being funny quickly.
