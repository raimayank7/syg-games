What a conversation starter is for

Most groups are busy and silent at the same time: full of forwarded pictures and greetings, with nobody actually talking to anybody. A forward asks nothing of the reader. A question does. The right one, on the right day, turns a list of contacts back into a group of people, and it costs the person who posts it ten seconds.

Choosing a set

Pick by the people, not by the question. The family set is written so that grandparents and children can answer the same question. The friends set leans on debates and shared memories. The office set stays away from everything personal. The new-group icebreakers can each be answered in one line by a stranger, and the batch set brings back the classroom. There is a Hindi set as well, for groups where Hindi is the language of the house. Every page also explains whom to ask first, what to avoid and how to bring the quiet members in.

Four habits that get replies

  • One question at a time. A list of ten gets none answered.
  • Answer it yourself first, briefly. A short, honest answer makes it safe for others.
  • Choose the hour. Sunday morning for families, the lunch break for teams, late evening for friends.
  • React to every answer. People write again when they feel heard.

If words fail, a poll is the easiest opener of all, because voting takes one tap. For a group that has only just been made, begin with the set-up checklist.
