Welcome messages for new group members

Ready messages to welcome a new member. A good welcome tells the person what the group is for and makes it easy to say hello.

Tap a message to copy it. Words in [brackets] are for you to change.

What a welcome message has to do

A person who has just been added to a group is, for a moment, a guest standing at the door of a room full of people talking. A good welcome does three things in three lines. It says the person's name, so the group knows who has arrived. It says in one sentence what the group is for, so the newcomer knows what is expected. And it gives one easy thing to do: say hello, tell us your flat number, read the pinned rules.

Choosing the right tone

Match the message to the group. A society or class group needs a clear, polite welcome with the practical facts. A friends' or cousins' group can tease a little, but only about arriving late, never about the person. An office group should be brief and should mention how the group is used. If several people join on one day, welcome them together in one message rather than filling the chat.

Small things that help

Ask the member's permission before adding them, where you can; nobody likes being dropped into a group of strangers. After the welcome, pause the ordinary chatter for a few minutes so that the newcomer's first hello is not buried. And if the new member is shy, a private "glad you joined, ask me anything" from the admin does more than any public message.
