Conversation starters for family groups

Questions that get parents, grandparents and children talking to each other instead of only forwarding messages. Post one on a Sunday morning.

Or tap any question to copy it.

Why questions work better than greetings

Most family groups are full of messages and empty of conversation. A forwarded picture asks for nothing, so nobody answers it. A question about the family's own life does the opposite: only these people can answer it, and each answer invites another. Ask grandfather what his first salary was, and within an hour an uncle has corrected him, a cousin has asked what it could buy, and the children have learned something no schoolbook holds.

Whom to ask first

Aim the first few questions at the elders. They hold the stories, they have the time, and they are often the quietest members because typing is slow for them. Tell them a voice note is welcome. Then use questions that put children and grandparents on the same level, such as favourite sweets, the best holiday or the funniest school memory. Save the answers that matter; a family group is, without anyone planning it, a family archive.

Questions to keep away from

Avoid anything that compares (marks, salaries, who visits more often), anything about marriage plans or children for the younger members, and any old dispute over property or money. If a question accidentally opens a sore subject, thank everybody and post a lighter one. A poll is a good change of pace, and Hindi questions are यहाँ.

How to get replies

  • Post one question at a time, not a list.
  • Answer it yourself first. A short, honest answer invites others.
  • Choose a time when people are free: Sunday morning for families, lunch break for teams.
  • React to every answer. People reply again when they feel heard.
