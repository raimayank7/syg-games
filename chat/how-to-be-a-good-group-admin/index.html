How to be a good group admin

An admin is not the owner of a group. An admin is its host: the person who makes sure that everybody knows why they are there and feels safe to speak. These ten habits make the difference between a group people mute and a group people open first.

Ten habits of a good admin

  1. Write the purpose in one line. Put it in the group description: "Society matters only" or "Family news and photos". Every later decision becomes easier when you can point to that line.
  2. Keep the rules short and pinned. Five to nine rules, in plain words. Ready sets are on our group rules pages.
  3. Welcome every new member. One friendly line and a pointer to the rules. People who are welcomed behave like guests; people who are ignored behave like strangers.
  4. Follow the rules yourself, first. If the admin forwards a joke in a "no forwards" group, the rule is dead.
  5. Correct in private, thank in public. A quiet personal message works better than a public scolding, and it saves the person's dignity.
  6. Never take sides in a fight. Stop the argument, not one of the people. See how to stop fights.
  7. Have a second admin. You will travel, fall ill or simply be busy. Choose someone calm, not someone popular.
  8. Use the tools. Polls for decisions, a pinned message for the rules, admin approval for new members in open groups, and "only admins can send" during announcements.
  9. Remove rarely, but really. Two warnings and then a short removal, the same for your best friend as for a stranger.
  10. Start conversations. A group with only announcements dies. Post a question or a poll once a week.

What an admin is not

  • Not a judge of who is right in a family quarrel.
  • Not a 24-hour help desk. Set quiet hours and keep them yourself.
  • Not responsible for making everyone happy. A clear rule, applied fairly, is enough.
