Conversation starters for friends groups

Questions for a friends' group that has gone quiet. They start arguments of the best kind, the ones nobody wins.

Or tap any question to copy it.

For the group that has run out of things to say

Friends' groups go quiet for a simple reason: life gets busy, and "what's up?" has only one answer, "nothing much". A good question skips the news and goes straight to opinion and memory, where friends are never short of material. Which of us would survive longest without a phone? What is the worst plan we ever made? Nobody needs to have had an interesting week to answer.

How to use these

The questions here are of three kinds. Debates have no right answer and split the group down the middle; post them when people are free to argue. Memories bring back shared stories and work best late in the evening. "Who among us" questions tease the whole group at once; use them only when everyone is in a good mood. Post one, give your own answer, and then stay out of the way.

A word of care

Old friends know each other's tender spots, so skip questions that corner one person about a job, a relationship, money or weight. If a friend is having a hard time, a private message is worth more than any group game. And when a debate gets heated for real, end it with a poll and accept the result; our guide on stopping fights covers the rest.

How to get replies

  • Post one question at a time, not a list.
  • Answer it yourself first. A short, honest answer invites others.
  • Choose a time when people are free: Sunday morning for families, lunch break for teams.
  • React to every answer. People reply again when they feel heard.
