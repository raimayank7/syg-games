Office team group rules

Rules for a team group at work. They respect people's time and keep work messages away from midnight.

*Group rules* 1. This group is for work updates of this team only. 2. Working hours for messages: 9 am to 7 pm on working days. Urgent matters by phone call. 3. Put the subject in the first line. One topic, one message. 4. No replies of "ok", "done" and "noted" unless you are asked to confirm. 5. No confidential files, salaries or customer data here. Use the official system. 6. No forwards, jokes or festival images. Wishes are welcome in one line. 7. Disagree with the idea, not with the person. Feedback on performance is given in private. 8. People on leave need not reply. Do not tag them. 9. When you leave the team, the admin will remove you. It is not personal.

What a team group is, and is not

A team group on a chat app is quick, and that is both its use and its danger. It is good for "the client call is moved to 4", "I am running late" and "the file is uploaded". It is bad for decisions that must be remembered, feedback on a person's work, anything confidential, and instructions that need a record; those belong in email or in the company's official tools. The first rule of an office group is therefore about what goes elsewhere.

Respecting time

The second question is hours. A message at 11 pm may need no reply until morning, but the phone still buzzes on somebody's bedside table. Agree on working hours for the group and on what counts as urgent enough to break them. When the manager follows this rule, the team will; when the manager breaks it, the rule is dead, whatever the pinned message says.

Introducing the rules

In an office, rules are best proposed in a team meeting, briefly discussed, and then posted by the team lead or with the lead's clear support. Keep the tone practical, not strict. Add a line that the group is not the place for leave requests or complaints, and say where those go.

For the friendly side of the team, a separate tea-break group with a light name works well, and the office conversation starters are safe for both.

How to use these rules

  1. Copy the rules and change any line that does not fit your group.
  2. Post them in the group and pin the message, so that it stays at the top.
  3. Put the one-line version in the group description. Ready lines are on the descriptions page.
  4. When a rule is broken, remind the person privately first. See how to be a good admin.
