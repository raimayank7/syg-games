Tambola number caller and ticket maker

Call numbers from 1 to 90 at random, with no repeats, and print fresh Tambola (Housie) tickets. Free, with no app and no login.

–

Each ticket has 3 rows with 5 numbers in each row, in the usual columns (1 to 9, 10 to 19 ... 80 to 90). To play on WhatsApp, take a screenshot of each ticket and send one to each player.

How to use the caller

  1. Give every player a ticket. Make and print them above, or send screenshots.
  2. Tap "Call next number". The big number is the one to announce. The board keeps every number called so far.
  3. When someone claims a prize, check their ticket against the green numbers on the board.
  4. The game is saved in this browser, so the board is still there if you close the page by mistake. Tap "New game" to clear it.

Winning patterns

PatternWhat the player needs
Early fiveAny five numbers on the ticket
Four cornersThe first and last numbers of the top and bottom rows
Top lineAll five numbers of the first row
Middle lineAll five numbers of the second row
Bottom lineAll five numbers of the third row
Full houseAll fifteen numbers on the ticket

Tips for the person calling

  • Call slowly and twice. Say the number, pause, and say it again: "Four and five, forty-five." In a large hall, use a microphone.
  • Announce the prizes first. Tell everyone which patterns are being played and in what order before the first number.
  • Stop the game for every claim. Check the ticket against the board before calling the next number. A claim made after the next number has been called is usually not accepted; say so at the start.
  • Keep the board visible. Turn the screen towards the players or connect it to a television, so that anyone who missed a number can catch up.

Playing over a video call or a chat group

Make the tickets here, take a screenshot of each one and send one to every player privately. Share your screen so that the board is visible, or type each number in the group as you call it. Ask a claimant to send a photo of the marked ticket. For a family spread across cities, this is one of the few games in which every age can take part at the same time.

New to the game? Read how to play Tambola. We suggest playing for fun, sweets or small gifts, not for money.
