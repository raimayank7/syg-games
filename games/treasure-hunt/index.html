Treasure Hunt at Home

A chain of written clues leads players from one hiding place to the next, until the last clue takes them to a hidden prize. It is the star game of birthday parties.

Players
2 or more
Time
30 minutes, plus 20 to prepare
You need
Paper chits, a small prize
Ages
5 and above
Type
Games for kids

Who it suits and when to play

A treasure hunt at home suits children from five to twelve, alone or in small teams, and it turns a dull afternoon or a birthday into an adventure. The grown-up needs twenty minutes to hide the clues. For children who cannot read yet, draw the clues as pictures. It works in a flat, a house with a garden or a single large room.

How to play

  1. Choose six to ten hiding places and a final place for the prize.
  2. Write a clue for each place. The clue for place 2 is hidden at place 1, and so on.
  3. Hide the chits backwards: start at the prize and work towards the first clue, so that nothing is missed.
  4. Hand the first clue to the players, or to each team in a different colour of paper.
  5. Players read a clue, run to the place, find the next chit and go on.
  6. The first team to reach the prize wins. Share the treat with everyone.

Clues you can use

  1. I have hands but cannot clap. Look behind me for your next step.
    Answer

    The wall clock

  2. I am full of cold air and keep your milk fresh.
    Answer

    The fridge

  3. You rest your head on me every night.
    Answer

    A pillow

  4. I show you your face but never smile first.
    Answer

    The mirror

  5. I drink water every morning but never get taller on my own.
    Answer

    A plant pot

  6. Shoes sleep here when they come home.
    Answer

    The shoe rack

  7. I have a spine and many leaves, but I am not a tree.
    Answer

    A book

  8. I wash your clothes and spin till I am dizzy.
    Answer

    The washing machine

  9. Open me to step outside. The next clue hangs on my inside.
    Answer

    The main door

  10. I keep your school things safe on your back.
    Answer

    The school bag

Tips and variations

  • For children who cannot read yet, draw the hiding places instead of writing.
  • Keep all hiding places low and safe: not near the stove, sockets, balconies or stairs.

Common problems and how to fix them

  • A clue is lost or found out of order. Number every clue, and keep a list of where each one is hidden.
  • The older child solves everything. Give each child a colour, and only clues of that colour count for them.
  • Children search in unsafe places. Say at the start which places hold no clues: the kitchen stove, the balcony railing, high shelves and locked cupboards.
