Party game timer

A big countdown for one-minute games and timed rounds. Choose a time and tap Start.

1:00

How to use the timer

Tap a time, or type your own number of seconds, then tap Start. The big numbers count down, the last ten seconds turn red, and a beep sounds at zero if your phone's sound is on. Pause holds the clock; Reset sets it back. Prop the phone where the players can see it, or cast your screen to a television for a large group.

The beep is made by your browser when you tap Start. Phones on silent may stay silent, so also call "Stop!" yourself.

Which time for which game

TimeGood for
30 secondsLightning rounds, tie-breakers, one clue in a word game
1 minuteStraw and Rajma, Biscuit on the Forehead, a turn in Don't Say It or Hum That Tune
3 minutesWrap the Mummy, Blindfold Makeover, planning time for a tower
5 minutesA round of table games at one station; writing cards
15 minutesBuilding in Newspaper Tower; the length of one whole game in your running order

Why time your games

  • Fairness. Every player gets exactly the same time, and nobody argues with a clock.
  • Excitement. A visible countdown makes the dullest task thrilling. Count the last five seconds aloud together.
  • Pace. A host who times each game finishes the party on schedule, with the cake cut before the youngest guests fall asleep.

One-minute games in a row

A set of "minute" challenges makes a good half hour: set the timer to one minute and run three or four table games back to back, with a new set of players for each. Keep a tally on the scoreboard. Change the order so that each team starts first once.

Tips

  • Keep the screen awake: your phone may dim after a while, so tap the screen now and then during long timers, or change the screen time-out in your phone's settings for the party.
  • For music games, use your music player's pause button instead; a timer makes the stop too easy to predict.
  • Children love being the timekeeper. It is a perfect job for a child who has just gone out of a game.
