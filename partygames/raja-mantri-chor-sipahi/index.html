Raja Mantri Chor Sipahi

The classic Indian chit game of bluff. Four folded chits decide who is the King, the Minister, the Thief and the Soldier. Only the King announces himself; the Minister must then work out, from faces alone, which of the other two is the Thief. It needs a scrap of paper and a pen, and it has filled power cuts and train journeys for generations.

The fun is in the acting. The Thief tries to look innocent, the Soldier tries to look just guilty enough to draw the Minister's guess, and everybody laughs when the Minister points the wrong way.

Ages
7 and up
Players
4 (or more with extra chits)
Time
15 to 20 minutes
Place
seated, anywhere
Energy
quiet
Group size
Small group (4 to 8)
Good for
All ages together, Children, Teenagers, Adults, Elders

What you need

Four small chits of paper, a pen, a sheet for the scores.

How to play

  1. Write Raja (1000 points), Mantri (800), Sipahi (500) and Chor (0) on four chits. Fold them the same way and toss them in the middle.
  2. Each player picks one chit and reads it secretly.
  3. The player who is Raja says aloud, "Mera mantri kaun?" (Who is my minister?).
  4. The Mantri answers, "Main, Maharaj," and is ordered to find the thief.
  5. The Mantri studies the two remaining players and points at one of them as the Chor.
  6. If the guess is right, everyone scores what is written on their chit. If it is wrong, the Mantri's 800 points go to the Chor for that round, and the Mantri scores nothing.
  7. Write down the scores, fold the chits and play again.

Who wins

After ten rounds, or any number agreed at the start, the highest total wins.

Variations and tips

  • For five or six players add chits such as Rani (900) and Senapati (700); they simply score their points.
  • Younger children enjoy saying the lines in a grand royal voice. Encourage it.
  • Change the point values if you like, but keep the Chor at zero.

Safety and kindness

Fold all chits identically; a chit with a torn corner will be recognised by the second round.

Common problems and how to fix them

  • The same child keeps getting Chor and is upset. Remind everyone that the Chor wins 800 points whenever the Mantri guesses wrong; it is the most exciting chit to hold.
  • Players peek at each other's chits. Have everyone hold the chit under the table and look down once.
  • Arguments about the score. One person, not playing that round or simply the eldest, keeps the sheet.

Party plans that use this game

  • Birthday party, ages 7 to 10
  • Family get-together, all ages
  • Festival gathering (Diwali, Lohri, New Year)
  • Small flat or rainy day

Host tools

Time the round with the party timer, make chits or random teams with the chit maker, and keep the points on the scoreboard.
