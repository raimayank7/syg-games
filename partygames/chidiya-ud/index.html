Chidiya Ud

"Chidiya ud" means "bird, fly". The leader calls out the names of things, and the players lift a finger only for things that really fly. It sounds too simple to be a game, and then a grandmother lifts her finger for "bhains ud" (buffalo, fly) and the whole room is laughing. It is the perfect opener for a mixed group of small children and elders, because it needs no materials, no moving about and no reading.

Ages
4 and up
Players
3 to 15
Time
5 to 10 minutes
Place
seated, anywhere
Energy
quiet
Group size
Small group (4 to 8), Medium group (9 to 20)
Good for
All ages together, Children, Elders

What you need

Nothing. A table or the floor to rest a finger on.

How to play

  1. Everyone sits in a circle and places one forefinger on the table or floor.
  2. The leader also has a finger down and calls quickly, "Chidiya ud!" raising their finger. Everyone raises a finger, because a bird flies.
  3. The leader goes on: "Tota ud! Kauwa ud! Mez ud!" The leader lifts a finger every time, to trick the players.
  4. Players must lift for things that fly and keep still for things that do not.
  5. Anyone who lifts for a table, a cow or a shoe, or fails to lift for a parrot or a kite, is out, or gives a forfeit such as singing one line of a song.
  6. The last player left becomes the next leader.

Who wins

The last player who has made no mistake wins.

Variations and tips

  • Speed is everything. Start slowly for small children and get faster.
  • Add tricky ones: aeroplane, kite, mosquito, balloon, hen (not really), time (it flies!). Agree before the game how to judge the doubtful ones.
  • Play it in English as "Birds fly", or in any language of the house.
  • For a big group, do not send people out; count mistakes and let the one with fewest win.

Safety and kindness

Forfeits must be kind: a song, a rhyme, three claps. Nothing that embarrasses a child.

Common problems and how to fix them

  • Everybody just copies the leader's finger. That is the game. Tell them to listen, not to look, and play one practice round.
  • Arguments about whether something flies. The leader's word is final for that round; then change the leader.

Party plans that use this game

  • Birthday party, ages 4 to 6
  • Family get-together, all ages
  • Senior citizens' get-together

Host tools

Time the round with the party timer, make chits or random teams with the chit maker, and keep the points on the scoreboard.
