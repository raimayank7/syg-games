Taste and Tell

Blindfolded players taste a tiny spoonful from each of eight or ten bowls and name what it is. Everyday foods become mysteries when you cannot see them: people confuse besan with sattu, jaggery with brown sugar, and everyone is astonished by how hard it is to tell apple from raw potato. It is a calm, seated game that fits well just before a meal.

Ages
6 and up
Players
4 to 20
Time
15 minutes
Place
a table
Energy
quiet
Group size
Small group (4 to 8), Medium group (9 to 20)
Good for
Children, Teenagers, Adults, Elders, All ages together, Ladies' parties, Office

What you need

Eight to ten foods in small numbered bowls, a clean spoon for every taste (or use toothpicks), a blindfold, paper and pen for the scorer, drinking water.

How to play

  1. Before the party, prepare the bowls where players cannot see them. Good choices: sugar, salt, jaggery, tamarind, roasted chana, poha, grated coconut, saunf, a slice of apple, a slice of pear, plain curd, a cube of paneer.
  2. Blindfold the first player and seat them at the table.
  3. Give a small taste from bowl 1 on a clean spoon. The player says what they think it is. The scorer notes the answer without saying whether it is right.
  4. Go through all the bowls, with a sip of water between tastes.
  5. Send the player to a "finished" side of the room so that they cannot tell the others, and bring in the next.
  6. When everyone has played, announce the answers.

Who wins

The player with the most right answers wins.

Variations and tips

  • Smell version: cotton wool or small covered cups with cardamom, coffee, hing, lemon peel, camphor, rose water, ginger. No tasting at all, and no allergy worries.
  • Touch version: a cloth bag or "feel box" with a potato, a comb, a key, a walnut, a sponge.
  • Team version: teams of three confer in whispers.

Safety and kindness

Ask every player about allergies and dietary rules before choosing the foods, and tell them the list will contain nothing unpleasant: no chillies, nothing spoiled, no tricks. Vegetarian items only unless you know the group. Never share a spoon.

Common problems and how to fix them

  • Players who have finished whisper answers. Keep them at the far end with a snack.
  • Scores are all the same. Add two hard items, such as two kinds of dal flour.

Party plans that use this game

  • Family get-together, all ages
  • Festival gathering (Diwali, Lohri, New Year)
  • Senior citizens' get-together

Host tools

Time the round with the party timer, make chits or random teams with the chit maker, and keep the points on the scoreboard.
