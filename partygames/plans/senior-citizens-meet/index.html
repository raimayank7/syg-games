Senior citizens' get-together plan

For
8 to 30 elders
Length
About one hour, with tea
Games
5

Games for elders should be played seated, at an easy pace, with plenty of room for talk. The best ones draw on what the players know better than anyone: old songs, sayings, tastes and stories. Gentle movement of arms is welcome; bending, hurrying and loud bangs are not. Keep the tone respectful: these are games for adults who happen to be older, not for children.

The running order

TimeGameNotes for the host
0 to 10 minChidiya UdA warm-up for hands and attention. Everyone knows it.
10 to 25 minHum That TuneSongs of their own youth. Let each tune lead to a memory; do not rush.
25 to 40 minSeated Balloon VolleyballGentle exercise for arms and shoulders, and a great deal of laughter.
40 to 50 minTaste and TellThe smell version is easiest: spices and flowers in covered cups.
50 to 60 minCoin in the TumblerFrom a chair beside the bucket. Calm concentration to finish.

Timings are a guide. Stop any game while people still want more, and drop one if you are running late.

What to get ready

  • Sturdy chairs with arms, in a circle with room behind
  • Song slips in large print
  • Balloons and a string
  • Covered cups of spices
  • A bucket on a low stool, a steel glass, coins
  • Tea, water and a microphone if the room is large

Comfort first

  • Good light and large print for anything that must be read.
  • Speak clearly and repeat instructions; seat those who hear less well near the host.
  • Never hurry a turn.
  • Check diets before any food game; many elders manage diabetes.
  • Offer every game as optional. Watching and commenting is taking part.
  • Ask them to teach a game. Many elders know chit games, cowrie games and rhymes that the young have never seen. An afternoon where they are the teachers is the best plan of all.

The games in this plan

  • Chidiya Ud 5 to 10 min
  • Hum That Tune 20 min
  • Seated Balloon Volleyball 15 min
  • Taste and Tell 15 min
  • Coin in the Tumbler 10 min
